\section{Background of the Invention}

\subsection{Field of the Invention}

\para The present invention relates to hardware-rooted cryptographic device identity, hardware identity element design, single-channel bidirectional communication protocols, line coding, true-random-number generation, write-once cryptographic storage, and domain-separated cryptographic attestation. More specifically, the present invention pertains to a chip architecture wherein the chip's permanent cryptographic identifier originates from physical entropy sampled by noise-detection circuitry resident on the chip's silicon at the unrepeatable moment of an owner-initiated provisioning event, the sampled entropy comprising transient physical phenomena present at and within the chip's silicon at the moment of sampling, the unrepeatability of the entropy deriving jointly from the owner's choice of triggering moment and from the unrepeatable physical state present at that moment; is never transmitted, exposed, or otherwise made accessible to any party outside the chip by any non-destructive means; and functions only as a key for cryptographic commitments produced inside the chip and emitted to requesting applications as domain-separated derivatives carried over a single bidirectional communication channel, the channel comprising any of an electrical conductor, an optical fiber coupled to paired light emitters and detectors, a magnetic or inductive coupling element, a capacitive coupling element, a radio-frequency near-field link, or an acoustic link, such that the chip's identifier has no issuing authority and the chip's external behaviour does not enable cross-application correlation of the device.

\para The present invention does not address human identity, user authentication, account systems, or service authorization. Those concerns are the subject of the related TOKEN application identified above and are outside the scope of the present filing.

\subsection{Description of the Related Art}

\para A purchaser of an integrated-circuit hardware identity device does not, in current commercial practice, purchase the device's cryptographic identity. Every hardware identity element commercially distributed in the past fifteen years has a cryptographic key whose value was known to the chip's manufacturer prior to the purchaser's acquisition of the chip, or whose value is subject to certification by a certificate authority controlled by the chip's manufacturer, or both. Commercial implementations of this pattern include the Apple Secure Enclave, the Google Titan and Titan M security chips, the Microsoft Pluton security processor, the ARM TrustZone CPU operating mode, the Qualcomm QSEE environment, the Intel SGX enclave system, and Trusted Platform Modules conforming to the Trusted Computing Group TPM 2.0 specification. In each such implementation, the chip's manufacturer occupies a position in the trust path during identity creation, attestation signing, certificate authority operation, or destruct command authority. The user, who purchased the chip and holds the chip physically, does not occupy any of these positions in the trust path.

\para A second structural problem affects prior art devices independently of the first. Even when the chip's manufacturer were entirely honest, and even when the manufacturer never used or disclosed its knowledge of the chip's key, the chip in prior art implementations answers cryptographic identity queries using a single fingerprint-stable key. Two unrelated services querying the same prior art chip with the same question receive answers that any third party can correlate, by inspection of the answers, without requiring access to either service's secret material. The chip thereby functions as a structural surveillance surface. Cross-service correlation of users does not require a manufacturer's cooperation; it requires only that the operators of multiple services maintain logs of the chip-attested values they have received, and that those logs be joined on the fingerprint field. The FIDO2 specification family partially addresses this property within FIDO authentication flows thru the use of per-relying-party credential key pairs, each registration with a relying party generating a fresh key pair independently of any prior relying party's key pair, such that two relying parties holding the same authenticator's public keys cannot correlate them by inspection; however, the authenticator's attestation statement to its hardware identity is signed by an attestation key whose root certificate is manufacturer-controlled, returning manufacturer trust to the path. Furthermore, the per-relying-party credential isolation is achieved by separately generating each key pair rather than by cryptographic domain separation of a shared internal secret, and so does not extend to uses of the device's identity outside FIDO authentication flows.

\para The pattern is consistent across the prior art. Chip identity, as the prior art implements it, is a permission granted by the chip's manufacturer and revocable by the chip's manufacturer; the chip's user is a tenant in the manufacturer's identity infrastructure rather than the owner of the chip's identity. Furthermore, prior art chip identity answers every cryptographic query with a structurally correlatable value, rendering the chip a single fingerprint regardless of the consent of the chip's user to such correlation.

\para Specific prior art systems and their limitations are summarized as follows. The Apple Secure Enclave provides per-chip unique identifiers generated at fabrication and signed by Apple's certificate authority; Apple retains provisioning records, can revoke device attestation, and the single per-chip identifier enables fingerprint correlation across all services that receive Apple-attested device assertions. Google Titan and Titan M security chips provide functionally equivalent vendor-attested keys under Google's certificate authority, with the same structural properties. Trusted Platform Modules conforming to TPM 2.0 provide an endorsement key (EK) generated at manufacture and certified by the TPM vendor; the EK cannot be replaced; the EK certificate identifies the manufacturer and the chip model; the Storage Root Key and Attestation Identity Keys are derived from the EK; and the manufacturer's signature serves as the trust root for all subsequent attestations.

\para ARM TrustZone provides a CPU operating mode, not a physically isolated enclave, and therefore shares physical resources (caches, branch predictors, memory buses, and power rails) with the rich execution environment; this property has resulted in repeated side-channel compromise (including the Foreshadow, Spectre, and \AE PIC vulnerability families). Microsoft Pluton is provisioned by original equipment manufacturers under Microsoft attestation, exhibiting the same vendor trust path structure. Intel SGX enclaves are attested by the Intel Attestation Service; the SGX system has been compromised via side channels and was structurally retired from Intel's consumer chip product lines in 2021, with the consequence that every user who had based identity infrastructure on SGX had that infrastructure revoked by Intel as a corporate decision.

\para FIDO2 hardware token products including YubiKey, Solokey, Trezor, and Nitrokey, provide per-device cryptographic keys generated on the device; however, the firmware controlling the device is signed by the device vendor, the attestation root certificate is vendor-controlled, and the per-relying-party key separation operates only within FIDO authentication flows. For any non-FIDO use of the device key (including TPM remote attestation, automotive vehicle identity binding, and IoT device registration), the single-fingerprint property of the underlying device key returns.

\para Hardware identity element products including the STMicroelectronics STSAFE family, the NXP A71CH, the Infineon OPTIGA family, and the Microchip ATECC family, provide factory-injected keys and vendor-signed device certificates. The chip ships with the key already provisioned by the manufacturer.

\para Single-wire bidirectional communication prior art (including the Dallas / Maxim 1-Wire family and the Philips I${}^2$C bus) provides physical-layer prior art for single-conductor bidirectional signaling but does not include cryptographic protocols, domain separation mechanisms, entropy semantics in the handshake, or silent-on-corruption behaviour at the wire level. Line-coding prior art for direct-current balance and clock recovery (including Manchester encoding, 8b/10b coding, and NRZ-I) requires dedicated overhead bits and explicit encoder and decoder hardware, and does not deliver protocol semantics within the line code itself.

\para Cryptographic prior art for elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman ephemeral-static handshake (including the Signal Protocol, the Noise Protocol Framework, and WireGuard) provides individual instances of any one of the five protocol functions claimed in the present invention's leading-key field, but does not combine all five protocol functions within a single transmitted field at the wire level. Cryptographic prior art for keyed cryptographic commitment functions (including BLAKE3 keyed hash, HKDF, HMAC, and the Poly1305 authenticator) provides commitment function prior art alone, but does not combine such functions with hardware-isolated permanent identifiers wherein use of the commitment function is enforced at the silicon level rather than the software level.

\para Physical Unclonable Function (PUF) prior art (including Verayo Vera M4H, Intrinsic ID SRAM PUF technology, and Maxim PUF-based cryptographic cells) provides hardware-rooted identity derived from manufacturing-process variation. PUF identities in commercial use are typically read out at provisioning time and enrolled in a vendor-controlled database, the stated purpose of the enrolment being to enable subsequent vendor certification of the chip's identity; the structural effect of such enrolment, irrespective of stated purpose, is that the vendor retains a queryable record of the chip's identity and therefore remains in the trust path. SRAM PUF embodiments operated without enrolment retain the single-fingerprint property and do not provide domain separation.

\para Trusted Computing Group Direct Anonymous Attestation (DAA) provides a cryptographic scheme allowing a Trusted Platform Module to prove possession of a vendor-signed key without disclosing the key, and provides some unlinkability between attestations. The DAA scheme requires a vendor-issued Endorsement Key as input; the vendor's signature serves as the trust root; and cross-issuer correlation remains possible thru the vendor's view of issued endorsements.

\para A further architectural axis distinguishes the prior art systems from the present invention. Identity-creation events in prior art chips occur either at the chip's manufacturing facility (where the manufacturer's controlled environment permits binning out mid-burn failures and presents the chip to the end user only in a fully-committed state) or by derivation from manufacturing-process variation as in PUF embodiments (where no burn event occurs and the identity is born continuously from first power-on). Owner-initiated burn-time identity creation, in which the chip's owner triggers the identity-creation event in the field on a bench that may be under adversarial power-supply control, faces an atomic-commit problem that the prior art does not address: the chip must present only valid named states at every instant during and after the identity-creation event, including under arbitrary external power interruption, because no controlled facility is available to bin out mid-burn failures and no operator-side recovery procedure may be relied upon. Prior art systems that achieve atomicity by externalizing the identity-creation event to a controlled facility re-introduce the vendor trust path the present invention rejects; prior art systems that achieve atomicity by deriving identity from manufacturing variation require either vendor-controlled enrolment or single-fingerprint operation, neither of which is compatible with the vendor-free domain-separated identity property the present invention requires.

\para All identified prior art systems share two foundational architectural assumptions: first, that a party other than the chip's owner participates in the trust path during identity creation; and second, that the device answers identity queries with a structurally correlatable fingerprint, regardless of whether partial unlinkability schemes operate above the wire layer. The present invention rejects both assumptions at the silicon level.

\section{Brief Summary of the Invention}

\para The present invention provides a chip and protocol for vendor-free domain-separated cryptographic device attestation. The chip's permanent identity credential is generated from physical entropy captured by noise-detection circuitry resident on the chip's silicon at the moment of an owner-initiated one-time provisioning ceremony, denoted herein as the \emph{whakaira}. The unrepeatability of the entropy derives jointly from the owner's choice of triggering moment, the choice being unpredictable to any party other than the owner, and from the transient physical phenomena present at and within the chip's silicon at the moment of triggering, the phenomena including thermal noise, shot noise, quantum mechanical fluctuations, and ambient electromagnetic and temperature conditions. The captured entropy is conditioned thru an on-chip cryptographic conditioner into the chip's permanent identifier and burned into write-once storage internal to the chip. The permanent identifier itself is never transmitted on any external interface of the chip, in plaintext or in any reversible encoding; subsequent external attestations are cryptographic commitments derived from the permanent identifier under a domain identifier supplied by the requesting application, the commitments being preimage-resistant against recovery of the permanent identifier. No external party witnesses the ceremony, signs the resulting identity, or holds a record of it. The chip's permanent identity therefore has no upstream issuing authority and exists nowhere except inside the chip in which it was created.

\para The chip's external interface comprises exactly one bidirectional communication port. In an electrical embodiment, the port comprises a single bidirectional input/output pin coupled to a single conductor; in an optical embodiment, the port comprises a paired light emitter and light detector coupled to a single optical fiber; in a magnetic or inductive embodiment, the port comprises one or more coupling coils; and in capacitive, radio-frequency near-field, and acoustic embodiments, analogous bidirectional coupling structures apply. The protocol carried on the port uses a mirror-XOR line code wherein both endpoints encode each transmitted bit as the exclusive-or of an own-idle bias, a transmit-data value, and the most recently decoded bit received from the other endpoint. The line code produces automatic direct-current balance, free clock recovery, and asymmetric drive that enables tristate idling without bus contention. A leading-edge framing convention announces every message with a single binary one bit transmitted as the channel's transition out of the idle state into active transmission, said leading bit serving as the frame edge for receiver synchronisation. The N-bit message payload follows immediately. Trailing binary zero bits of the payload are not actively transmitted; the channel returns to its idle state by passive biasing once the last actively driven binary one bit has been transmitted, and the receiver reconstructs trailing zeros by slot-counting against the known message length and decoding non-actively-driven slots as binary zero. The framing cost is therefore exactly one channel slot per message irrespective of payload length, and the framing savings are the trailing zeros of the payload that the sender omits. The channel elapses the payload length plus one slot of time per message in all cases; the framing never introduces a second dedicated framing slot. The decode operation is a slot-counted shift register requiring no framing-symbol detection logic. The per-message channel format places an ephemeral session key at the leading position of every payload, simultaneously providing key transport, decryption authority, message authentication, frame leading edge, and direct-current balance: five protocol functions delivered by one transmitted field.

\para External attestations are produced as keyed cryptographic commitments over the chip's permanent identifier and a domain identifier supplied by the querying application. Two queries with distinct domain identifiers produce cryptographically independent attestations whose correlation requires an information-theoretically infeasible preimage attack on the commitment function. The chip's permanent identifier is not exposed in any attestation. Cross-application correlation of the device is therefore not structurally enabled by the architecture; correlation requires the chip's voluntary participation in producing attestations for both correlating domains, which in turn requires user-mediated consent.

\para The chip maintains two distinct identifiers: a permanent identifier denoted \emph{ira} (burned once during the provisioning ceremony) and a per-session secret denoted \emph{wairua} (established once per power-on cycle, at the first handshake following reset, by the chip's noise-detection circuitry sampling transient physical phenomena present at and within the chip's silicon at the moment of the handshake, and irrecoverably lost upon any interruption of the chip's power supply). Neither identifier alone authorizes operations requiring proof of identity-plus-liveness. A handshake bit from the host doubles as a true-random-number-generation request; the resulting fresh entropy constitutes the chip's response. No separate ``request entropy'' command exists in the protocol.

\para The chip's permanent identifier is stored as multiple cryptographic-inverse pairs in write-once cells, with byzantine quorum required at each chip reset. The provisioning ceremony is atomic with respect to interruption of the chip's external power supply: the chip contains an energy buffer (in some embodiments, an on-die or on-package capacitance; in other embodiments, an external capacitor coupled to the chip's supply pin) and a sensor measuring stored energy against a threshold sufficient to drive the entire identifier-burn sequence to completion at the required burn voltage with margin, and the chip's finite-state machine refuses to begin the burn until the sensor reports that threshold met. Once the burn begins, the energy buffer is isolated from the external supply and supplies the burn to completion regardless of any subsequent external interruption. The entropy-gathering and cryptographic-conditioning sub-steps preceding the burn operate on volatile registers only and write no storage cell; interruption during those sub-steps drops the volatile state and the chip presents the unprovisioned KORE state at next reset. The ceremony therefore presents exactly two protocol-visible states at every instant: KORE before burn-commit, and ORA after burn-completion; no intermediate state is reachable by external power interruption at any time. Single-event upset, manufacturing defect, electromagnetic disturbance, and other post-provisioning corruption are absorbed silently below the byzantine quorum-failure boundary. Below quorum, or upon any cryptographic verification failure, true-random-number-generation health failure, or non-operational condition of the chip's finite-state machine, the chip enters the NGARO state, wherein wire output is gated off and the wire's idle behaviour is indistinguishable from a chip not physically present. No error code, failure indicator, or distinguishing signal is emitted on the wire under any failure condition.

\para The chip's only clock source is a free-running ring oscillator internal to the chip. The chip has no external clock input, no host-authenticated destruct command, no firmware update path, no method of in-circuit identity replacement, and no in-system mechanism by which an attacker possessing the chip can extract its permanent identity, force it to use entropy from any source other than the chip's noise-detection circuitry sampling at the moment of the triggering event, or substitute a different identity. State transitions are forward-only and enforced by silicon physics rather than by software.

\para The resulting device-identity primitive is application-agnostic. Downstream use cases include, but are not limited to: Internet-of-Things device identity without manufacturer involvement after initial provisioning; hardware wallets without factory-injected keys; user-sovereign identity binding as described in the related TOKEN application; automotive secure boot identity surviving ownership transfer; medical device patient-private binding; industrial control vendor-independent attestation; and defense supply-chain post-fabrication substitution detection.

\subsection{Terminology}

\para The present specification uses certain te reo M\=aori terms for the chip's identity primitives, ceremony events, and operational states. A typographic convention distinguishes the two categories of term: names set in capital letters (KOHI, HORO, BLAKE3, TATARI, TAHU, KORE, ORA, HARA, WHARA, NGARO, and the message designator Q\_HEALTH) denote finite-state-machine states and protocol constants of the chip's implementation, following the hardware-description-language convention of capitalised state identifiers; names set in lowercase italics (\emph{whakaira}, \emph{ira}, \emph{wairua}, \emph{motuhake}) denote the language-level primitives themselves (the ceremony, the identifiers, and the test domain), which exist independently of any particular state machine that implements them. The terms are used with the following meanings: \emph{whakaira} denotes the ceremony in which the chip's permanent identifier is brought into existence from physical entropy captured by noise-detection circuitry resident on the chip's silicon at the moment of an owner-initiated triggering event, the te reo M\=aori word formed by prefixing the causative \emph{whaka-} to \emph{ira} and signifying the bringing of the \emph{ira} into being; the \emph{whakaira} is owner-initiated, irreversible, and witnessed only by the chip itself. \emph{ira} denotes the permanent identifier resulting from the \emph{whakaira}, and serves as the chip's lifelong device identity. \emph{wairua} denotes the per-session secret drawn from fresh physical entropy at the first handshake following power-on reset, persisting unchanged in volatile registers until power interruption, and is distinct from \emph{ira}. KOHI denotes a sub-step of the \emph{whakaira} comprising gathering entropy from on-chip true-random-number-generation primitives. HORO denotes a sub-step of the \emph{whakaira} comprising chaotic-dynamical amplification of the gathered entropy thru a fixed-point iterated map with data-dependent operation selection, the te reo M\=aori word signifying swift motion, a landslide, or to swallow whole, and reflecting the rapid information-destroying mixing performed by the stage. TATARI denotes a sub-step of the \emph{whakaira} comprising a randomized pre-burn delay whose duration is derived deterministically from the conditioned identifier candidate, the te reo M\=aori word signifying waiting or delay; the sub-step is interposed between cryptographic conditioning and write-once burn to defeat timing-predictable power-cut attacks on the burn. TAHU denotes a sub-step of the \emph{whakaira} comprising writing the conditioned entropy to write-once storage. KORE denotes the chip's pre-provisioning operational state; the te reo M\=aori word signifies the primordial void or absence of form. ORA denotes the chip's fully operational state; the te reo M\=aori word signifies the condition of being alive and well, and corresponds to the chip with all four redundancy pairs verified. HARA denotes a single-fault-tolerated operational state; the te reo M\=aori word signifies a breach, fault, or error, and corresponds to the chip with three redundancy pairs valid and one pair invalid. WHARA denotes a two-fault-tolerated operational state at minimum byzantine quorum; the te reo M\=aori word signifies a bruise or injury, and corresponds to the chip with two redundancy pairs valid and two pairs invalid. NGARO denotes the silent failure state; the te reo M\=aori word signifies the condition of being lost, vanished, or silenced. \emph{motuhake} denotes a canonical test domain identifier used in protocol exercises.

\para The chip's five operational state codes are designated as follows. KORE designates the state in which the chip's write-once storage cells are all zero, no \emph{whakaira} has been performed, and the chip has no identity. ORA designates the state in which the \emph{whakaira} has completed and the chip's identity is recoverable from all four redundancy pairs. HARA designates the state in which one redundancy pair has become invalid and the chip's identity is recoverable from the remaining three valid pairs. WHARA designates the state in which two redundancy pairs have become invalid and the chip's identity is recoverable at minimum byzantine quorum from the remaining two valid pairs. NGARO designates the state entered upon any of the following conditions: byzantine quorum failure of the storage, cryptographic verification failure, true-random-number-generation health failure, or any non-operational condition of the chip's finite-state machine. In the NGARO state, the wire output is gated off and the wire's behaviour is indistinguishable from that of a chip not physically present.

\para The four wire-reportable state codes are encoded as a two-bit field with the numeric value equal to the chip's vitality on the four-pair scale, with the value increasing as the chip's identity-attesting capability increases. The encoding is: KORE = binary 00, WHARA = binary 01, HARA = binary 10, ORA = binary 11. The NGARO state is not assigned a numeric code because the chip in NGARO state does not transmit on the wire; the receiving host infers NGARO from the absence of any response within the protocol's timeout window, the absence being indistinguishable at the wire from a chip not physically present.

\subsection{The Provisioning Ceremony}

\para The chip is shipped from the manufacturer to the owner in the KORE state. The chip's write-once storage cells are all zero. The chip's finite-state machine reports a chip\_state value of KORE. The chip has no permanent identity in this state. The chip cannot produce an identity-bearing attestation in this state because no \emph{ira} has been written; the chip remains able to draw \emph{wairua} from its noise-detection circuitry and to emit fresh entropy in response to a handshake, those operations being independent of \emph{ira}. The chip's manufacturer, having shipped the chip in this state, possesses no information concerning the chip's identity, because the chip's identity does not exist.

\para An owner of the chip initiates the \emph{whakaira} by transmitting a structured attestation-request message to the chip's external bidirectional pin under the protocol's framing convention, namely a leading-edge frame bit followed by the message payload. The chip's finite-state machine accepts the request only after the receiver has decoded a complete, well-framed protocol message; transient electrical disturbances on the pin, including electrostatic discharge events, capacitive coupling artefacts, and isolated edges arising from connector insertion or wire handling, do not satisfy the framing convention and are rejected by the receiver without state transition. The \emph{whakaira} trigger is specifically the first permanent-mode attestation request received while the chip is in the KORE state: a well-framed request whose cryptographic invariant, described in connection with the identity dichotomy below, selects the permanent identifier as the active commitment key. Session-mode requests, handshakes, and state queries received while the chip is in the KORE state are served from \emph{wairua} in the ordinary way and do not trigger the \emph{whakaira}. When the chip is in any provisioned state, a permanent-mode attestation request is served as an ordinary attestation without re-entering the \emph{whakaira}. The energy-buffer threshold gate described separately herein operates as an independent second condition: the finite-state machine refuses to begin the TAHU burn until the on-chip energy sensor reports stored energy above the burn-completion threshold, regardless of the framing-validated request having been received.

\para In the KOHI state, the chip's noise-detection circuitry samples transient physical phenomena present at and within the chip's silicon at the moment of the sampling. The phenomena include, in various embodiments, thermal noise (Johnson-Nyquist noise arising from the silicon's instantaneous temperature), shot noise (arising from the quantization of electron flow at the moment of sampling), quantum mechanical fluctuations in metastable resolution, ambient electromagnetic field conditions, and other transient environmental physical noise sources present at and within the chip. The unrepeatability of the resulting entropy derives jointly from the owner's choice of triggering moment, the choice being unpredictable to any party other than the owner, and from the unrepeatable physical state present at that moment. The chip's noise-detection circuitry comprises, in some embodiments, a plurality of free-running ring oscillators sampled thru statistical entropy-extraction tests including, in some embodiments, the repetition count test, the adaptive proportion test, and an iterated derivative test specified by NIST Special Publication 800-90B; in some embodiments, one or more transition-effect ring oscillators; in some embodiments, one or more metastable arbitration circuits; in some embodiments, one or more subthreshold-inverter shot-noise samplers or thermal-noise amplifiers; or in some embodiments, any combination of the foregoing operated in parallel. The combination of detection circuits is a matter of implementation choice; the requirement is that the sampling be performed by noise-detection circuitry resident on the chip's silicon, and that no entropy be supplied as protocol data thru the chip's external communication interface. The chip's noise-detection circuitry necessarily transduces physical phenomena originating outside the silicon, including ambient temperature, ambient electromagnetic field conditions, and external mechanical or acoustic disturbances coupling into the silicon; such environmental coupling is the physical mechanism by which the entropy arises and is not excluded. What is excluded is any pathway by which an external party could deliver bits of intended entropy to the chip as a payload over the chip's external bidirectional pin, the chip's protocol providing no command, register, or state in which the on-chip conditioner would accept bits arriving at the pin as a substitute for, or contribution to, the entropy captured by the on-chip noise-detection circuitry.

\para The chip gathers entropy until on-chip health diagnostics confirm sufficient entropy quality. If the health diagnostics report failure, the chip enters the NGARO state and the \emph{whakaira} is aborted. In this case, the chip silently refuses to provision rather than provisioning with insufficient entropy. The chip cannot be coerced into substituting attacker-supplied bits for its on-chip noise sources, because the chip's protocol provides no pathway by which bits arriving at the external pin reach the on-chip conditioner. If the silicon's true-random-number generator is broken, the chip refuses to claim an identity it cannot defensibly attest under.

\para Upon successful completion of KOHI, the finite-state machine transitions to the HORO state. In this state, the entropy gathered in KOHI is fed to a chaotic-dynamical amplifier comprising a fixed-point iterated map over a plurality of state buckets, in some embodiments sixteen buckets each carrying a sixteen-bit fraction and an eight-bit exponent, mixed across a plurality of rounds, in some embodiments sixteen rounds. Each round applies to each bucket one operation selected from a menu of distinct lightweight arithmetic-logic operations by an index that is itself derived from the bucket's current value, said data-dependent operation selection producing a combinatorial path explosion that complements the data-dependent shift amounts and the per-round round-constants. A subset of the operation menu is information-lossy, in some embodiments approximately fourteen of thirty-two operations being lossy, such that the amplifier cumulatively destroys on the order of several hundred to several thousand bits of internal state across its rounds, said destruction occurring within a state space populated by entropy substantially in excess of the destruction bound. The HORO stage thereby preserves a high lower bound on retained min-entropy while ensuring that any partial recovery of the stage's output is computationally precluded from inverting to the stage's input, and produces a chaotically mixed intermediate value as input to the cryptographic-conditioning sub-state that follows. The chaotic amplifier circuit is part of the chip's mask design and operates on volatile registers only.

\para Upon successful completion of HORO, the finite-state machine transitions to the BLAKE3 state. In this state, the chaotically mixed intermediate value emerging from HORO is concatenated with the original entropy gathered in KOHI, and the concatenation is conditioned thru an on-chip keyed cryptographic hash function implemented in hardware logic. In some embodiments, the hash function is BLAKE3 operating in keyed mode. The output of the conditioner is a 256-bit cryptographic-quality value, deterministic with respect to the gathered entropy and resistant to bias from any non-uniformity in the entropy source. The conditioner circuit is part of the chip's mask design and is therefore known to the chip's manufacturer; however, the manufacturer does not know the conditioner's input on any specific chip, because the input is the entropy gathered in KOHI on that specific chip and the chaotic transform performed in HORO on that specific chip.

\para Upon completion of BLAKE3 conditioning, the finite-state machine transitions to the TATARI state, holding the conditioned identifier candidate in volatile registers for a randomized delay before the burn. The burn-onset moment is therefore unpredictable to an external party, and a supply interruption cannot be scheduled to coincide with the burn. Upon expiry of the delay, the finite-state machine evaluates the energy-buffer threshold gate before isolating power and committing to TAHU.

\para The energy-buffer threshold gate is the final precondition of the TAHU burn. The chip's mask design incorporates an energy buffer coupled to the chip's supply rail and a sensor measuring the buffer's stored energy. In some embodiments the buffer comprises an on-die or on-package capacitance; in other embodiments the buffer comprises an external capacitor coupled to the chip's supply pin and dedicated to the burn. The sensor's threshold is selected to exceed the integrated energy required to drive the complete TAHU write sequence (comprising the programming pulse at the required burn voltage applied sequentially or concurrently to every storage cell of the inverted-pair layout) by a design margin sufficient to absorb worst-case process, voltage, and temperature variation in the burn-cell programming current, plus a further margin against measurement uncertainty in the sensor itself. Upon expiry of the TATARI delay, the finite-state machine samples the sensor and refuses to transition to the TAHU state unless the sensor reports stored energy above the threshold. If the sensor reports stored energy below the threshold, the finite-state machine drops the conditioned identifier candidate from its volatile registers and presents the KORE state at next reset, the chip's owner being free to re-attempt the \emph{whakaira} after replenishing the energy buffer; no write-once storage cell is touched and no information about the discarded candidate persists. If the sensor reports stored energy above the threshold, the finite-state machine performs two electrically simultaneous actions: it isolates the energy buffer from the chip's external supply rail thru a switch element in the chip's mask design that prevents subsequent external supply interruption from draining the buffer, and it transitions to the TAHU state. The isolation switch remains engaged until the TAHU sequence completes. The combined effect of the threshold-with-margin check and the supply isolation is that, once TAHU begins, the burn is electrically guaranteed to run to completion against any external power interruption strategy: the buffer alone supplies the burn, the buffer was verified to contain more energy than the burn consumes plus margin, and the buffer is no longer connected to anything an attacker can pull down.

\para In the TAHU state, the conditioned 256-bit value is written to the chip's write-once storage cells according to the inverted-pair layout described in connection with \figref{storagearray}. The write operation is electrically irreversible: the cells transition from an unwritten state to a written state, and the chip's mask design provides no path by which they can return to the unwritten state. Isolation during TAHU is limited to the write-once storage section and its burn-current path; the remainder of the chip continues to operate from the energy buffer or external supply and may continue to service \emph{wairua}-based handshake and boot-only attestation requests on the external pin during the burn, said requests being independent of the stored identifier. Device-specific attestation, which requires the stored identifier, becomes available only upon the FSM's subsequent transition to the ORA state. The stored identifier is at no time transmitted on the external pin in plaintext or in any reversible encoding; ORA-state responses to device-specific attestation requests are cryptographic commitments derived from the stored identifier under a domain identifier supplied by the requesting application, said commitments being preimage-resistant against recovery of the stored identifier.

\para Upon completion of TAHU, the finite-state machine transitions to the ORA state. The chip is provisioned. The chip's identity exists. The chip will not reveal the identity to any external party, including the chip's owner, the chip's manufacturer, or any authority that may compel disclosure at any future time. The identity is a quantity inside silicon that no party outside the silicon observes.

\subsection{The Single Bidirectional Interface}

\para The chip's external interface comprises exactly one bidirectional communication port. The chip does not include a separate clock input, a debug interface, a JTAG tap, an in-circuit firmware update mechanism, an out-of-band manufacturer access path, a destruct port, or any other interface that crosses the chip's package boundary. The architectural decision to provide a single bidirectional port is not aesthetic; each interface absent from the chip is a class of attack absent from the chip.

\para The physical embodiment of the single bidirectional port may vary by application. In an electrical embodiment, the port comprises a single bidirectional input/output pin coupled to a single conductor, with one or more pull-down resistors (or, in some embodiments, one or more pull-up resistors) coupled to the conductor establishing the conductor's idle electrical state. A single such resistor on the shared conductor is sufficient; in some embodiments, for symmetry of the two endpoints' analog interfaces, a respective pull-down resistor is provided at each endpoint's pin, optionally in series with a respective current-limiting resistor, the parallel combination establishing the same idle state. The number and placement of the biasing resistors is an analog implementation choice and does not alter the line-code semantics, which depend only on the conductor settling to the idle level whenever no endpoint actively drives. In an optical embodiment, the port comprises a paired light emitter and light detector at the chip side and a corresponding paired emitter and detector at the host side, with both pairs coupled to a single optical fiber operating as a bidirectional channel; the channel's idle state is established by an optical bias source or by passive emitter quiescence with detector thresholding. In a magnetic or inductive embodiment, the port comprises one or more coupling coils, with the channel operating thru near-field magnetic coupling and the channel's idle state established by a quiescent flux level. Capacitive, radio-frequency near-field, and acoustic embodiments employ analogous structures. References herein to ``wire,'' ``conductor,'' ``drive,'' ``pull-down,'' ``high logic level,'' ``low logic level,'' and similar electrical terminology are used to describe the line code clearly in the electrical embodiment; in non-electrical embodiments, the corresponding analog structures and signal states apply, and the term ``channel'' is used herein when the description applies generally to any physical embodiment.

\para The absence of a JTAG interface eliminates in-system invasive probing of the chip's internal state. The absence of a firmware update mechanism eliminates the possibility of future vendor-pushed identity replacement. The absence of a debug interface eliminates any manufacturer access path designated as a service mode. The absence of a destruct pin eliminates any host-authenticated wipe that an attacker possessing the chip could trigger. The absence of an external clock input eliminates clock-glitching attacks against the chip's finite-state machine.

\para The chip's only clock source is a free-running ring oscillator internal to the chip. The frequency of the ring oscillator varies with temperature, voltage, and process variations; the protocol described in the present specification tolerates this variation thru automatic clock recovery in the line code. The host endpoint does not share an external clock signal with the chip. The host endpoint derives the chip's slot timing from observing the chip's wire transitions during the heartbeat phase of the protocol. A single chip can therefore be installed in any host that implements the protocol, regardless of the host's own clock, without provisioning a clock relationship between the chip and the host.

\para The chip's finite-state machine transitions are forward-only. State transitions are enforced by combinational logic in the chip's mask design that physically does not provide a backward transition. The transition from KORE to KOHI is unidirectional. The transition from KOHI to HORO is unidirectional. The transition from HORO to BLAKE3 is unidirectional. The transition from BLAKE3 to TATARI is unidirectional. The transition from TATARI to TAHU is unidirectional. The transition from TAHU to ORA is unidirectional. The NGARO state, once entered, is recoverable only by power-on reset and only if the underlying failure condition is no longer present. The chip's mask design provides no software-controlled path by which an attacker could cause the chip to traverse its state graph in reverse, nor any path by which an attacker could leave the chip in a state partially committed between two adjacent FSM states. The pre-burn sub-steps (KOHI, HORO, BLAKE3, TATARI) operate on volatile registers only and present the KORE state at next reset if interrupted; the TAHU sub-step is gated by the energy-buffer threshold and supply-isolation arrangement described above and runs to completion against any external supply manipulation once entered; no other transition writes persistent state. Consequently the chip exposes exactly two persistent post-reset states to any external party: KORE (no \emph{ira} ever committed), and a quorum-determined operational state (ORA, HARA, WHARA, or NGARO) reflecting the committed \emph{ira}. No external action, including power glitching, line glitching, electromagnetic injection, or any combination, can drive the chip into a persistent intermediate or partially-burned state.

\subsection{The Mirror-XOR Line Code}

\para The mirror-XOR line code carries the present protocol over the single bidirectional channel coupled to the chip's external port. The description below is given for the electrical embodiment; the same logic applies to optical, magnetic, inductive, capacitive, radio-frequency, and acoustic embodiments with corresponding signal states substituted. Time on the channel is divided into uniform slots whose ownership alternates between the two endpoints. Each endpoint maintains a data stream of bits intended for transmission; in idle the data stream is constant zero, and activity is signalled simply by inserting non-zero bits into the stream. In its own slot, an endpoint computes wire\_out as the exclusive-or of three values: own\_idle, own\_data, and other\_last\_data, where own\_idle is a fixed per-endpoint constant (binary 1 at the chip endpoint, binary 0 at the host endpoint), own\_data is the next bit in the endpoint's own data stream, and other\_last\_data is the bit most recently decoded from the other endpoint. The endpoint actively drives the channel HIGH if wire\_out evaluates to 1, and releases the channel to its passive idle level (the pull-down resistor in the electrical embodiment) if wire\_out evaluates to 0; the other endpoint never drives during this slot, so the channel state in any slot has a single defined source. In the other endpoint's slot, an endpoint decodes the observed channel value as the exclusive-or of three values: the observed wire value at the bit's sample point, the other endpoint's idle constant other\_idle, and own\_last\_data, the bit the endpoint itself most recently transmitted.

\para The asymmetric drive convention has the following consequences. With both data streams at constant zero, the chip endpoint computes wire\_out = 1 and drives HIGH in its slots, while the host endpoint computes wire\_out = 0 and releases the channel to its pull-down LOW in its slots; the channel idle pattern is therefore the deterministic alternating sequence $1,0,1,0,1,0,\ldots$, a self-clocking heartbeat carrying 100\% transition density for clock recovery without any active host contribution. The chip endpoint transitioning from HIGH to LOW within one of its own slots (produced by the chip inserting a 1 into its data stream, which under the mirror-XOR rule with stream-idle preconditions evaluates to wire\_out = 0 and a release) constitutes the message-start edge. No separate framing symbol is transmitted; a 1 in the data stream is itself the frame edge. The same applies to the host, whose insertion of a 1 produces wire\_out = 1 in a host slot, breaking the idle LOW with an active HIGH drive. Both directions therefore operate full-duplex with framing emerging directly from the data, and the XOR compensation against other\_last\_data restores direct-current balance within at most a two-slot transient following any inserted 1.

\para The drive logic at each endpoint computes a drive-enable signal as the logical AND of the endpoint's own slot ownership indicator and the endpoint's computed wire output value. Each endpoint drives the wire to a high logic level when the drive-enable signal is asserted. Each endpoint releases the wire to a high-impedance state when the drive-enable signal is deasserted. The wire is held at a low logic level in the absence of drive by an external pull-down resistor coupled to the conductor. Neither endpoint drives the wire to the low logic level. Bus contention between the endpoints during simultaneous transmission slots is structurally impossible because both endpoints' slot ownership indicators cannot be simultaneously asserted.

\para The line code produces three properties without dedicated hardware support. First, direct-current balance over the wire emerges from the encoding. Over any sequence of slots in which the transmitted bits are statistically uniform, the wire's mean voltage is approximately centred between the high logic level and the low logic level. The ephemeral session keys of the per-message wire format ensure such statistical uniformity. No bias resistor, decoupling capacitor, or explicit balance encoding is required.

\para Second, free clock recovery emerges from the encoding. Each transition in the wire's state is a clock edge for the receiving endpoint. In the heartbeat phase of the protocol, the wire alternates between the high and low logic levels at the slot rate, providing the receiver with a transition per slot for clock synchronisation. During data transmission, the statistical randomness of the data ensures that wire transitions occur with sufficient density to maintain receiver clock lock. The clock recovery hardware comprises one logic gate and a slot counter; no dedicated phase-locked loop, delay-locked loop, or explicit synchronisation pattern is required.

\para Third, the asymmetric drive property enables silent-on-corruption behaviour. The chip endpoint never drives the wire to the low logic level. Gating off the chip's drive-enable signal causes the wire to fall to the low logic level under the action of the external pull-down resistor. The chip does not actively transmit a silence signal; the chip merely stops driving. This property enables the NGARO state to produce wire behaviour indistinguishable from the chip's physical absence: the chip stops driving, the wire returns to its pull-down idle, and an external observer cannot distinguish a chip that is present but has stopped driving from a chip that is not present.

\para Each endpoint samples the channel state at a sampling rate substantially higher than the slot rate and accumulates the samples across the slot width by means of an up/down counter. The counter is incremented on each sample of the channel at the high logic level and decremented on each sample of the channel at the low logic level. At the slot boundary the receiving endpoint decodes the slot's bit value as the sign of the accumulated counter: a positive accumulated value decodes as binary 1, and a non-positive accumulated value decodes as binary 0. The counter is then reset for the next slot. For binary-valued channel samples, the sign-of-sum operation is mathematically equivalent to a majority vote across the samples of the slot, and is therefore equivalent to a median filter of width equal to the number of samples per slot. The receiver consequently tolerates noise events shorter in duration than one half of the slot width without bit error, irrespective of the noise event's amplitude, because such noise events flip fewer than one half of the slot's samples and therefore cannot change the sign of the accumulated counter. The sampling architecture comprises one up/down counter and one sign-bit register per endpoint; no analog filter, equalizer, or feedback loop is required. The same architecture is used at both endpoints.

\para The per-message wire format places an ephemeral session public key at the leading position of every authenticated message. The same field simultaneously performs five protocol functions that conventional cryptographic protocols deliver via separate fields.

\para The first function is cryptographic key transport. The ephemeral session public key is the sending endpoint's contribution to an ephemeral-static key agreement protocol. In some embodiments, the key agreement protocol is elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman over Curve25519, such that the sender publishes a fresh X25519 public key and the receiver combines that public key with the receiver's long-term private key to derive a shared session key.

\para The second function is decryption authority. The session key derived from the key agreement of the first function is used as the symmetric key for authenticated encryption with associated data (AEAD) of the ciphertext that follows the ephemeral session public key in the message. The presence of the ephemeral session public key in the message authorizes the receiver to derive the decryption key.

\para The third function is message authentication. The authenticator tag produced by the AEAD operation of the second function (in some embodiments, a Poly1305 tag) is computed over the ephemeral session public key and the ciphertext under the session key. Any modification of either the ephemeral session public key or the ciphertext invalidates the authenticator tag and is detected by the receiver.

\para The fourth function is the frame leading edge. A single binary one bit is transmitted before every message as the wire's transition out of the idle state into active transmission, the receiver synchronising message-start detection to said transition. The leading frame bit is not a bit of the message payload; it is one additional channel slot per message regardless of payload length, occupying the slot immediately preceding the first bit of the ephemeral session public key field.

\para Concomitantly, trailing zeros of the message payload are not actively driven onto the channel. The sender ceases active transmission after the last actively driven binary one bit of the payload, and the channel returns to its idle low state by passive biasing. The receiver reconstructs the trailing zeros by slot-counting N slots after the leading frame bit, where N is the known fixed payload length, and decoding each non-actively-driven slot as binary zero. The decode operation is therefore a slot-counted shift register without dedicated framing-symbol detection logic: each slot is sampled, and any slot not actively driven by the remote endpoint is decoded as zero.

\para The combination of the leading frame bit with the omitted-trailing-zeros convention bounds the framing overhead at one channel slot per message, and the channel elapses the payload length plus one slot of time in all cases. The framing never introduces a second dedicated framing slot beyond the leading frame bit; in particular, no trailing end-of-message marker, no length prefix, and no inter-message gap signaling is present in the wire-level protocol. The same channel-idle state at the end of transmission also serves as the silent-on-corruption indicator described in the failure-mode subsection: a chip in the NGARO state and a chip having completed transmission both present an undriven channel; the receiver disambiguates by message-length accounting and timeout, not by additional wire signaling.

\para The fifth function is automatic direct-current balance. The ephemeral session public key, being the output of a cryptographically uniform process such as X25519 public key generation from a random scalar, has an approximately uniform bit distribution. The 256 bits of the ephemeral session public key contribute approximately 128 high transitions and 128 low transitions to the line code, satisfying the direct-current balance requirement without a dedicated balance encoding field.

\para No prior art cryptographic protocol combines all five of the foregoing functions in a single transmitted field at the wire level. Individual protocols provide subsets of the five functions thru separate fields. The combination of all five functions in one transmitted field, with the ephemeral session public key serving as the universal carrier, is novel.

\subsection{Domain-Separated Per-Application Attestation}

\para The chip produces an attestation in response to a query received from a requesting application. The query includes a domain identifier supplied by the requesting application. The domain identifier may be any value the application selects, such as a service uniform resource locator, an application name, a randomly generated handle, a hash of the application's identity, or a domain-specific identifier such as a use-case label. The chip does not constrain the structure of the domain identifier.

\para The attestation produced by the chip is a keyed cryptographic commitment derived under an active commitment key and computed over an input that includes the domain identifier supplied by the requester. The active commitment key is selected from among \emph{ira} and \emph{wairua} according to the identity dichotomy described in a subsequent section. The active commitment key is not consumed by a single cryptographic hash function alone, but by the same cascaded HORO-plus-BLAKE3 conditioner described in connection with the provisioning ceremony: the active key, concatenated with the supplied domain identifier and any session-specific freshness inputs, is first passed thru the chaotic-dynamical amplifier (HORO) for chaotic mixing with information-loss, and the amplifier's output is then concatenated with the original input and conditioned thru a keyed cryptographic hash function (in some embodiments BLAKE3 operating in keyed mode) to produce the 256-bit attestation. The cascaded conditioner is used in lieu of a single hash construction so that the security of the attestation against recovery of the active commitment key does not rest on the preimage-resistance of any one cryptographic primitive in isolation; an adversary attempting to recover the active key from a published attestation must defeat both the chaotic-dynamical amplifier's path-explosion plus information-loss combination and the cryptographic hash's preimage-resistance, the two being independent security arguments.

\para The requesting application verifies the attestation by recomputing the keyed cryptographic commitment under a previously-established public commitment derived from the chip. The chip's public commitment is a 256-bit value derived from the chip's permanent identifier and shared at the time the chip was first registered with the application. The public commitment does not reveal the chip's permanent identifier; it is sufficient for the application to verify subsequent attestations produced by the chip.

\para Two queries with distinct domain identifiers produce cryptographically independent attestations. To correlate the chip across two such queries, a third party would be required to compute one query's attestation from the other's, which requires either a preimage attack on the keyed cryptographic commitment (which is information-theoretically infeasible against cryptographically secure commitment functions including BLAKE3) or the chip's voluntary participation in producing both attestations. The chip produces an attestation only in response to a query that supplies the corresponding domain identifier. The chip does not produce attestations spontaneously. The chip's user controls which queries are delivered to the chip. Cross-application correlation of the chip is therefore not a structural property of the architecture; correlation requires the user to consent to allowing both correlating applications to query the chip.

\para This property distinguishes the present invention from prior art device-identity primitives commercially available in 2026. Trusted Platform Module endorsement keys, Apple Secure Enclave unique identifiers, Google Titan keys, and per-chip identifiers from hardware identity element manufacturers including STMicroelectronics, NXP, Infineon, and Microchip, answer device-identity queries with values that any third party can correlate by inspection. The present invention answers device-identity queries with values that are cryptographically independent across distinct domain identifiers. The privacy property is enforced at the silicon level rather than by a policy layer above the silicon.

\para The Q\_HEALTH state-query exchange is a specific application of the in-session messaging mechanism, distinct from the attestation mechanism described above. Q\_HEALTH is a request for the chip's current operational state vector. It is not an attestation, does not invoke \emph{ira}, does not reveal the chip's permanent identity, and produces a response of the same wire format and the same size as any other in-session message. The Q\_HEALTH response carries the chip's packed operational state vector (including the two-bit chip\_state field and any further per-redundancy-pair validity bits) within the response payload, the payload being authenticated and bound to the active session by the same chain-state mechanism that authenticates every other response in the session. A verifying application correctly receives the state vector only by decoding the response against its expected session chain state; if the application's chain state has diverged from the chip's, or if an attacker has interposed an impersonating endpoint, the decode yields uniformly distributed garbage indistinguishable from a corrupted message and the application takes its ordinary session-corruption recovery path. The chip's identity is bound to the session at the time of session establishment, by the attestation mechanism described in the preceding subsection; Q\_HEALTH inherits that binding without re-establishing it per query, which is what permits the response to remain identical in size to any other in-session message rather than carrying a per-response identity commitment. The Q\_HEALTH embodiment illustrates that in-session state reporting and per-application attestation are independent mechanisms in the present protocol: an application that has already established a session with a given chip does not need to repeat the attestation step to obtain trustworthy state, and conversely a Q\_HEALTH response carries no information usable by any party that has not already established the session.

\subsection{The Permanent-Plus-Session Identity Dichotomy}

\para The chip maintains two distinct identifier values inside the chip. The first identifier, \emph{ira}, is generated by the provisioning ceremony described above and is stored in the chip's write-once storage. The second identifier, \emph{wairua}, resides in the chip's volatile registers. \emph{Wairua} is established once per power-on cycle, at the chip's first handshake following reset, by the chip's noise-detection circuitry sampling transient physical phenomena present at and within the chip's silicon at the moment of the handshake. Once established, \emph{wairua} persists unchanged for the full power-on lifetime of the chip; subsequent handshakes within the same power-on cycle deliver fresh entropy to the host and refresh per-message session chain state, but do not replace \emph{wairua}. The chip's volatile registers, including those holding \emph{wairua}, lose their contents irrecoverably upon any interruption of the chip's external power supply, the chip's mask design providing no battery, no on-die retention element, and no mechanism by which \emph{wairua} can persist across a power-on reset.

\para \emph{Wairua} is held in the volatile registers using the same inverted-pair discipline as the permanent identifier: a plurality of register groups, in some embodiments four, each holding \emph{wairua} in one register and the bitwise complement in the paired register, verified at use by the same exclusive-or validity check applied to the write-once storage groups. The mirrored layout converts single-event upsets of the volatile registers into detected validity failures rather than silent corruption. Upon a validity failure of the \emph{wairua} groups during a power-on cycle, the chip ceases responding on its external interface for the remainder of the power-on cycle; recovery requires interruption of the chip's power supply followed by a fresh handshake, both endpoints re-deriving session state in lock-step. The chip performs no silent re-derivation of a corrupted \emph{wairua}, because session state derived from a silently re-derived secret would diverge undetectably from the host endpoint's session state; the forced power cycle converts the corruption into an observable session break.

\para The two identifiers carry complementary properties, each insufficient on its own and each providing a distinct security guarantee in combination. \emph{ira} is permanent and identical across every session of the chip's life; \emph{wairua} is fresh per session and stable within a session, and is cryptographically independent of any other session's \emph{wairua}. The properties that follow from each identifier's behaviour are therefore distinct.

\para \emph{ira} alone, used as the sole key for attestations, is replay-vulnerable: any attestation the chip produces under \emph{ira} alone may be captured and presented again to any application that holds the chip's public commitment, with no mechanism distinguishing a fresh attestation from a stored one. Without per-session freshness, attestations under \emph{ira} alone are durable artifacts subject to lift-and-reuse attacks.

\para \emph{wairua} alone, used as the sole key for attestations, provides freshness but not cross-session identity. Across two distinct sessions, the \emph{wairua} values are independent samples drawn from the chip's noise-detection circuitry and conditioned to cryptographic-quality output, and are by construction unlinked to each other and to any persistent quantity. A verifying application receiving a \emph{wairua}-keyed attestation in session $A$ and a \emph{wairua}-keyed attestation in session $B$ can derive no relation between the two from their attestation values, and therefore cannot conclude that the producer of attestation $A$ is the same chip as the producer of attestation $B$; any true-random-number generator coupled with the same conditioner could have produced statistically indistinguishable values.

\para Within a single session, however, \emph{wairua} is stable, and this stability is itself a security-relevant primitive of independent utility. A verifying application that issues the same query to the chip at two distinct points in time during the same session receives the same response, the response being a deterministic function of \emph{wairua} (constant within the session) and the query (identical between the two issuances). A subsequent identical query that returns the identical response is therefore cryptographic proof that the chip's volatile state has been continuously preserved between the two issuances: no interruption of the chip's external power supply has discharged the chip's volatile registers, and no replacement of the chip with a different physical chip has occurred. The same property holds in reverse: an identical query that returns a different response is cryptographic proof that the chip's session has been broken, whether by power interruption or by physical replacement. This session-continuity proof property is a per-session liveness primitive useful for, among other applications, continuous-trust attestation of long-running services on the chip's host (such as digital content rental or licensing, in which the licensor wishes to verify that the host's protected state has been continuously preserved since rental began), satellite or other remote-deployed device integrity audit (in which a ground station wishes to confirm a deployed device has not been reset, replaced, or maliciously rebooted since deployment), and tamper-evident continuous operation of high-assurance systems (in which any interruption of the chip's session evidences the tampering).

\para A permanent-mode attestation combines the two identifiers in a single derivation: the commitment is computed thru the cascaded HORO-plus-BLAKE3 conditioner over \emph{ira}, per-session freshness derived from \emph{wairua}, and the domain identifier supplied by the requester. The combination provides the security properties of each constituent: \emph{ira}'s permanent-identity binding ensures that attestations across sessions authenticate to a single chip's public commitment, and the \emph{wairua}-derived freshness ensures both that each session's attestations are unique to that session, defeating cross-session replay, and that intra-session repetition of a query yields the session-continuity proof described above. A session-mode attestation is keyed from \emph{wairua} and the session chain state alone and carries no permanent-identity binding.

\para The active commitment key for each specific exchange is selected by a cryptographic invariant carried in the host's message rather than by a plaintext mode indicator. In some embodiments, the cryptographic invariant comprises a comparison of a verification field in the host's message against two candidate expected values, one expected value being a keyed hash of the host's recovered message and the other expected value being the bitwise complement of the keyed hash, with the polarity of the matching candidate determining the active commitment key selection.

\para Selection of the active commitment key by cryptographic invariant rather than by plaintext mode indicator provides an additional privacy property. The mode of any specific exchange is part of the protected payload rather than part of the wire-observable framing. An attacker observing the wire cannot determine whether a given exchange used \emph{ira} or \emph{wairua} as the active commitment key without breaking the underlying cryptographic invariant. The same wire-indistinguishability property extends to the Q\_HEALTH state-query exchange described above and to every other category of in-session message defined by the protocol: every response shares the same wire format, the same payload size, and the same authenticator construction, so an attacker observing the wire cannot determine the semantic category of a given exchange, whether it is a per-domain attestation, a Q\_HEALTH state report, or any other in-session message, without breaking the cryptographic invariant. Message categories are therefore protocol-internal distinctions visible only to endpoints holding the session state, and present no wire-observable side channel by which an attacker could selectively target, filter, or correlate exchanges by semantic type.

\subsection{Hardware-Rooted Provable Erasure}

\para The session-continuity proof property described above admits a converse use of independent commercial and security significance: verifiable proof that a chip's session has ended, and that any cryptographic capability bound to the prior session's \emph{wairua} has been irreversibly lost. The present subsection describes the embodiment of this property as a primitive for hardware-rooted cryptographic erasure.

\para In the embodiment, a payload protected by a content-issuing application is encrypted under a content key derived in part from the chip's current-session \emph{wairua}. The derivation is performed by the cascaded HORO-plus-BLAKE3 conditioner described in connection with the attestation mechanism above, taking \emph{wairua}, the protected payload identifier, and any application-supplied freshness as inputs. The chip retains the derived content key only in volatile registers for the duration of the session; the derived key is not committed to any write-once storage cell, nor to any retention element in the chip's mask design, the chip having no battery, no on-die non-volatile memory other than the write-once \emph{ira} storage, and no path by which \emph{wairua} or any value derived from \emph{wairua} may persist across an interruption of the chip's external power supply.

\para Erasure of the protected payload is accomplished by interrupting the chip's external power supply for any duration sufficient to discharge the chip's volatile registers. No protocol-level command, no fresh-session handshake, and no other in-session signal removes \emph{wairua} or any value derived from \emph{wairua}; the only mechanism by which the prior \emph{wairua} can be lost is the physical discharge of the registers that hold it. In every such case, the prior \emph{wairua} is irrecoverably lost and the derived content key is irrecoverably lost with it. The chip's mask design, providing no mechanism by which the prior \emph{wairua} could be preserved, makes the loss of the derived key a physical consequence of the loss of volatile state rather than a software-asserted property; the chip is mechanically incapable of retaining the prior \emph{wairua} across a power interruption regardless of any external instruction the chip might receive, regardless of any modification an attacker might make to the chip's firmware, and regardless of any internal state the chip's finite-state machine might occupy.

\para The chip subsequently proves the erasure to the content-issuing application by responding to a session-continuity probe (comprising any deterministic query whose response is a function of \emph{wairua}) and producing a response that does not match the response previously recorded by the application from the prior session. The application's verification proceeds in two parts: an \emph{ira}-keyed attestation confirms that the responding chip is the same physical chip with which the application registered (the chip's permanent identifier being unchanged across the power interruption), and the mismatched session-continuity response confirms that the responding chip's \emph{wairua} has been replaced with a fresh sample (the prior session's volatile state having been lost). The combination of identity-match with session-mismatch is cryptographic proof that the same chip has undergone session-state loss between the two observations.

\para The combined proof requires no trust in the chip's firmware, no trust in the chip's manufacturer beyond the manufacturer's role in producing genuine hardware of the published mask design, and no trusted execution environment attestation chain. The erasure is enforced by the physical absence of any retention path in the silicon; the proof is constructed from primitives the chip exposes regardless of firmware state; and the verifier requires only the chip's previously-established public commitment derived at registration.

\para The above proof of erasure is conditional on the responding device being genuine hardware of the published mask design at registration time. The condition is satisfied in the present invention not by reliance on a vendor-signed endorsement, which would introduce an upstream issuing authority the chip's design otherwise refuses, but by reliance on the audited manufacturing process by which the chip is fabricated. The chip's mask design and fabrication process are intended for public publication and third-party audit; verification of any specific chip's hardware authenticity is therefore reducible to verification of the chip's provenance thru the audited supply chain. The architecture differs from prior art trusted-platform-module and secure-enclave constructions, in which proof of hardware authenticity reduces to verification of a vendor signature under a vendor endorsement key, said vendor key being a centralized cryptographic root subject to coercion of a single party. In the present invention, no such centralized root exists; the trust anchor is the audited supply chain, and coercion of the trust anchor requires coercion of every audited fabrication step rather than coercion of a single signing authority.

\para Embodiments of the hardware-rooted provable-erasure primitive include, without limitation: digital content licensing in which a licensor verifies that a licensee's playback device has irreversibly lost the capability to decrypt licensed content upon end-of-rental; lending of digital library media in which a return event is verified cryptographically rather than asserted by client software; tamper-evident continuous operation of high-assurance systems in which an unexpected loss of session state evidences interruption; secure transfer of capability between devices in which the source device's loss of capability is verified before the destination device's grant is honoured; and any analogous setting in which a remote party requires cryptographic confirmation that a counterparty's previously-held capability is no longer recoverable.

\subsection{The Silent-On-Corruption Failure Mode}

\para The chip's response to any failure condition is silence at the wire. The wire driver's drive-enable signal is gated off; the wire returns to its pull-down idle; and the chip emits no other signal at the chip's external pin.

\para Failure conditions detected by the chip's combinational logic include: a cryptographic verification failure on a received message; a byzantine quorum failure of the chip's identity storage; a true-random-number-generation health failure; and any non-operational condition of the chip's finite-state machine not enumerated above. The fourth condition is a catch-all for any internal state of the chip not anticipated as operational by the chip's mask design, including a bus stall, an undefined state code, or a coherency failure in the cryptographic conditioner.

\para The chip does not emit an error code, a status byte, a corruption indicator, or any other distinguishing signal at the wire under any failure condition. Any signal the chip emitted at the wire would be observable by any party with access to the wire. A chip that distinguished its failure modes thru its wire output would make its failure modes observable to parties from whom such information should not be available. An attacker who could distinguish a chip present but with failed byzantine quorum from a chip not present learns information about the chip's storage state; an attacker who could distinguish a chip with failed true-random-number-generation diagnostics from a healthy chip learns information about the chip's entropy source; an attacker who could distinguish a chip in NGARO due to cryptographic verification failure from a chip not in the system learns information about the chip's protocol exchange history. None of these distinctions assists the chip's owner; all of them assist an attacker.

\para The NGARO state collapses all failure conditions to a single externally-observable state: silence. The wire is at the low logic level. Whether the chip is in the ORA state and idle, in the NGARO state, unpowered, or physically removed from the system, the wire is at the low logic level. The chip's owner may distinguish among these conditions by means other than the wire (a power indicator, a physical inspection, or a recovery procedure); an external observer accessing only the wire cannot distinguish among them.

\para The implementation of the NGARO state at the wire driver comprises an AND gate computing the drive-enable signal as the logical AND of three signals: a chip-operational status signal, derived combinationally from the byzantine quorum check; a slot ownership indicator; and the endpoint's computed wire output value. When the chip-operational signal is at logic low, the drive-enable signal is at logic low, the wire driver does not drive the wire, and the wire is held at the low logic level by the external pull-down resistor. The mechanism is structurally simple and is enforced by the chip's mask design.

\subsection{Inverted-Pair Write-Once Identity Storage}

\para The chip's permanent identifier is stored in a plurality of write-once storage groups, each group comprising at least two cryptographically-related write-once cells. In some embodiments, each storage group comprises two cells, with the chip's permanent identifier stored in one cell of the group and the bitwise complement of the permanent identifier stored in the paired cell. In some embodiments, four such storage groups are provided.

\para The validity check applied to each storage group requires that the bitwise exclusive-or of the cells in the group equals the all-ones value, and that neither cell in the group is at the all-zero state. The first condition confirms that the cells contain cryptographic inverses. The second condition excludes the unprovisioned (KORE) state in which both cells are at the all-zero state, distinguishing a properly provisioned storage group from a never-provisioned storage group and from a partially provisioned storage group.

\para The byzantine quorum threshold in some embodiments is two valid storage groups out of a total of four storage groups, with all valid storage groups required to agree on the recovered identifier. In some embodiments, the threshold may be configured to other values based on the chip's intended deployment population and acceptable failure probability. The thresholds may be derived from population failure-rate calculations, including the calculation that, for an embodiment with four storage groups and a quorum of two, a per-pair failure probability of approximately 0.79 percent yields a whole-chip failure probability below 10${}^{-15}$, which is suitable for population-scale deployment.

\para The inverted-pair storage layout absorbs several classes of corruption silently below the quorum-failure boundary. A single-event upset, such as a cosmic-ray-induced bit flip in one cell of one storage group, invalidates that group thru the validity check, leaving the remaining groups operational; if three groups remain valid, the chip operates in the HARA state. A latent manufacturing defect that manifests after provisioning, such as a marginal eFuse cell or a cell with reduced retention, appears as a single-group invalidity and is treated identically to a single-event upset. An electromagnetic disturbance of sufficient magnitude to flip bits in one or more cells invalidates the affected groups; the remaining valid groups continue to support chip operation. A partial provisioning failure, in which power loss during the TAHU sub-step of the \emph{whakaira} leaves some storage groups burned and others kore, results in a mixed-kore-with-burned state that the classifier rejects as a security precaution; a chip whose provisioning was interrupted does not operate, because the identity it would attest under may not be the identity the owner intended to provision.

\para The integrity verification of the chip's permanent identifier is performed exclusively at read time by the byzantine quorum check, and no per-cell read-back verify is performed during the burn sub-step. This architectural choice is intentional. A per-burn read-back verify implementation would detect a defective storage cell at the moment of its attempted write and, having no recourse against a write-once cell that has failed to take, would necessarily abort the provisioning ceremony and place the chip in a permanently inoperable state on the first encounter with any cell-level defect. The chip's owner would observe such a chip as bricked from manufacturing-process variation that is not in fact systemic, the defect being statistically present in any large population of fabricated devices at the cell level even when no group of cells is jointly compromised. The byzantine quorum check, by contrast, operates over the full set of storage groups simultaneously and applies the failure threshold to the count of compromised groups rather than to the existence of any single compromised cell. A chip with a defective cell in storage group 0, when verified at read time, presents an invalidated group 0 and (assuming the remaining three groups burned correctly) a valid quorum of three groups; the chip is reported in the HARA state and is fully operational. The same chip, verified instead by per-burn read-back during TAHU, would have its ceremony aborted at the first slot and the chip would be permanently NGARO. Deferring verification from write time to read time therefore converts a class of manufacturing defects from chip-level failures into silently-absorbed group-level failures, increasing the yield of usable chips against the same defect rate. The architecture is intentionally more permissive at burn time and more rigorous at read time, the burn-time circuit having visibility only into the single cell being written while the read-time circuit has visibility into the entire storage array and may apply byzantine reasoning across the redundancy.

\para No external signal sequence at the chip's external port can alter, replace, or extract the contents of the write-once storage cells. The cells are coupled to internal silicon paths and to the chip's finite-state machine, but not to the external port in any path that exposes the cells' contents in plaintext or in any reversible encoding. The chip's permanent identifier is therefore non-destructively inviolable from outside, in the sense that an attacker possessing the chip and operating the chip's external interface has no means by which to extract, substitute, or modify the chip's permanent identifier. The permanent identifier participates in cryptographic operations inside the chip only as a key for cryptographic commitments; the commitments emitted at the chip's external port are functions of the identifier under preimage-resistant cryptographic hash, and an external party in possession of any quantity of such commitments cannot recover the identifier itself by any non-destructive computational means.

\para Recovery of the chip's permanent identifier by an attacker would require physical invasive analysis of the chip's silicon, including but not limited to: decapping of the chip package and layer-by-layer mechanical or chemical removal of the chip's metal layers to expose the write-once storage cells for optical or electron-microscope readout; focused ion beam milling to probe internal silicon nets; laminographic X-ray imaging of the chip's internal structure; or other destructive techniques. The chip's mask design may, in some embodiments, further frustrate such physical invasive analysis thru one or more anti-invasive features, including: a top-metal active mesh covering the write-once storage cells and the cryptographic conditioner, the mesh configured to detect penetration and cause the chip to enter the failure-response state described above; one or more passive shielding layers comprising metallization patterns configured to obstruct laminographic X-ray imaging of the chip's internal structure; an irradiation-detection circuit configured to cause the chip to enter the failure-response state upon detection of an attempt at invasive analysis using charged-particle or photon-based probes; one or more dummy circuit blocks resembling but not implementing the cryptographic functions of the chip, the dummy circuits configured to mislead reverse-engineering effort; and a tamper-evident package coating configured to render decapping observable to the chip's owner. The choice and combination of anti-invasive features is a matter of implementation; the protocol described in the present specification is independent of the chosen anti-invasive features and is enforced at the silicon level regardless of which combination is selected.

\subsection{Atomic Provisioning Commit}

\para The provisioning ceremony of the present invention is triggered by the chip's owner in the field, at a moment of the owner's choosing, on whatever bench or installation environment the owner happens to be using. The chip is not provisioned in a controlled manufacturing facility under the supervision of the chip's manufacturer; the chip is not binned out before shipment if its provisioning is incomplete; no operator-side recovery procedure may be relied upon to complete a partially performed provisioning. The chip must therefore present only valid named states of its finite-state machine at every instant during and after the provisioning ceremony, under arbitrary timing of external power interruption. This requirement is, in the present specification, the atomic provisioning commit property; it distinguishes the present invention from prior art identity-creation arrangements that achieve atomicity by performing the identity-creation event at the manufacturer's facility and that re-introduce the vendor trust path the present invention rejects.

\para The atomic provisioning commit property is implemented by an energy-budgeted burn-initiation gate. The chip comprises an energy storage element, a sensor measuring stored energy in the element against a configured threshold, and a finite-state machine that refuses to begin the burn of the chip's write-once storage cells until the sensor reports that the threshold is met. The threshold is configured to a stored-energy quantity sufficient, with engineering margin, to drive the entire burn sequence to completion at the required burn voltage. Once the burn begins, the energy storage element is electrically isolated from the chip's external supply by a switching element coupled between them, and the burn proceeds to completion under power supplied by the storage element regardless of any subsequent interruption of the chip's external power supply. Interruption of the external supply before the threshold is met leaves no storage cell written and the chip presents the KORE state on the next power-on cycle. Interruption after the burn begins is irrelevant because the burn is no longer dependent on the external supply.

\para The entropy-gathering sub-step (KOHI), the chaotic-dynamical amplification sub-step (HORO), the cryptographic-conditioning sub-step (BLAKE3), and the randomized pre-burn delay sub-step (TATARI) of the provisioning ceremony precede the burn-initiation gate and operate on volatile registers internal to the chip. No write-once storage cell is touched during these sub-steps. Interruption of the chip's external power supply during any of these sub-steps drops the volatile registers and the chip presents the KORE state at next reset; the conditioned identifier candidate that existed in the volatile registers at the moment of interruption is lost and is not recoverable by any party, and the chip's owner may re-attempt the ceremony, the re-attempt drawing a fresh sample of physical entropy at the moment of re-attempt and producing a different conditioned identifier. An adversary observing or interrupting any number of failed attempts therefore learns no information about the identifier eventually committed, because each successful commit binds to fresh entropy drawn at the moment of the successful attempt, said entropy being unrelated to the entropy of any prior interrupted attempt.

\para In a non-limiting embodiment, the chip's write-once storage cells comprise spin-transfer-torque magnetic-tunnel-junction (STT-MRAM) cells configured for parallel write. The complete burn of the inverted-pair storage array described in connection with \figref{storagearray} (eight 256-bit slots) requires approximately two nanojoules at approximately one volt over a duration of approximately one hundred nanoseconds. The energy storage element required is correspondingly modest; in a representative embodiment, the chip's intrinsic on-die and on-package power-supply decoupling capacitance, comprising on the order of ten nanofarads in typical packaging, exceeds the burn-energy budget by several orders of magnitude and itself serves as the energy storage element with no dedicated external component required. The energy sensor in this embodiment comprises a comparator measuring the chip's internal supply rail voltage against a reference, the comparator asserting the burn-permission signal when the rail voltage exceeds the threshold consistent with sufficient stored energy.

\para In a further non-limiting embodiment, the chip's write-once storage cells comprise electrically-programmable fuses whose burn voltage and energy requirements exceed the budget of intrinsic on-package decoupling. In this embodiment, the energy storage element comprises an external capacitor coupled to the chip's external supply pin (or to a dedicated burn-rail pin), the capacitor's capacitance selected to store, with margin, the energy required to burn the complete inverted-pair storage array at the eFuse burn voltage. In a representative embodiment of this kind, an external capacitor on the order of one millifarad coupled in conjunction with an on-die charge pump producing the eFuse burn voltage is sufficient. The energy sensor in this embodiment comprises a comparator measuring the external capacitor's terminal voltage against a reference. The switching element comprises a transistor or schottky diode coupled between the external supply and the capacitor, configured to disconnect the capacitor from the external supply at the moment burn begins.

\para In further non-limiting embodiments, the chip's write-once storage cells comprise embedded floating-gate cells, antifuses, mask-programmed read-only memory cells, polymer write-once cells, or any combination of the foregoing; the energy storage element comprises any combination of on-die capacitance, on-package capacitance, and external capacitance; and the energy sensor comprises any analog circuit measuring stored charge or rail voltage and emitting a single binary burn-permission signal. The atomic provisioning commit property is independent of the specific technology selected for the storage cells, the specific physical embodiment of the energy storage element, or the specific implementation of the sensor; the requirement is that the chip's finite-state machine refuse to begin the burn until the sensor confirms sufficient stored energy, and that the burn, once begun, complete under power supplied by the storage element rather than by the external supply.

\para Failure modes of the energy-buffered burn are absorbed by the same KORE-or-ORA two-state invariant. A chip whose energy sensor never reports the threshold met (because the external supply is insufficient to charge the storage element to threshold, or because the sensor is defective) never enters the burn state and therefore never writes any storage cell; the chip presents the KORE state indefinitely until a sufficient external supply is provided or the chip is replaced. A chip whose burn begins but whose energy storage element fails mid-burn (a fault outside the design margin, such as a cracked capacitor) leaves a partially-burned storage array; the chip's classifier behaviour with respect to a partially-burned array is determined by the byzantine quorum logic described in the preceding subsection, and depending on the extent of partial burn the chip may present any of the operational states (ORA, HARA, WHARA, or NGARO) on the next power-on cycle. This residual hazard is bounded by the engineering margin of the energy budget and is not, in any case, exploitable by an external attacker thru normal power-supply manipulation.

\para In some embodiments, additional reinforcement of the atomic-commit property is provided by structural arrangements complementary to the energy buffer. In one such embodiment, the chip further comprises an auxiliary single-bit commit cell electrically distinct from the inverted-pair storage groups, the commit cell being programmed by the chip's finite-state machine after the burn of the inverted-pair storage groups has completed; the byzantine quorum classifier disregards the contents of the inverted-pair storage groups unless the commit cell is in its programmed state. The inverted-pair storage groups and the auxiliary commit cell are not arranged in a hierarchy of priority among storage cells; the inverted-pair storage groups remain mutually equivalent peers under the byzantine quorum check, and the commit cell is an additional gating element separate from the byzantine quorum storage. In this complementary embodiment, the energy buffer ensures completion of the inverted-pair storage groups' burn, and the subsequently programmed commit cell provides a second layer of protection against residual partial-burn failure modes outside the engineering margin of the energy budget. The complementary embodiments are not required for the basic atomic-commit property of the present invention, which is provided by the energy buffer alone.

\para The chip's finite-state machine presents, under the energy-buffered burn architecture, exactly one of two protocol-visible states at every instant during and after the provisioning ceremony: KORE (burn-initiation gate not yet asserted, no identity stored) or ORA (burn complete, identity stored and recoverable by byzantine quorum). The HARA and WHARA degradation states remain reachable subsequently, on time scales of hours to years, by single-event upset, manufacturing defect, or other corruption of the storage cells in a chip whose burn was successfully completed; those degradation states are not reachable during the provisioning ceremony itself. No intermediate state between KORE and ORA is reachable by external power interruption at any time during the provisioning. The atomic-commit property is enforced by silicon physics and the energy budget rather than by software, and is independent of the chip's firmware, the chip's host endpoint, and any operator-side recovery procedure.

\subsection{Application Surface of the Device-Identity Primitive}

\subsection{Experimental Validation}

\para The protocol described in the present specification has been implemented and validated on hardware. A non-limiting example implementation comprises a Lattice ECP5-25F field-programmable gate array configured to perform the chip-side protocol, coupled by a single conductor to a Raspberry Pi RP2040 microcontroller configured to perform the host-side protocol. A nine-test simulation testbench exercises the chip-side finite-state machine thru the provisioning ceremony, two repeated permanent-mode authenticated exchanges (to demonstrate determinism within a session), a session-mode authenticated exchange (to demonstrate mode separation), a state-query authenticated exchange (to exercise the Q\_HEALTH state-vector reporting embodiment), and two single-bit-fault exchanges (to exercise the silent-on-corruption failure mode). All nine simulation tests passed without modification of the protocol implementation. A twelve-test host-side implementation crate exercises the host-side protocol and passes all twelve tests, including end-to-end exchange of the canonical test vector thru an in-process chip emulator. The chip-side cryptographic hash computation has been independently validated by cross-comparison against the host-side cryptographic hash computation on real silicon: a fingerprint of the chip's session pad observed on the field-programmable gate array's output display was matched exactly against the host-side computation, confirming that the byte-order convention of the cryptographic hash is independently validated on real silicon rather than only being self-consistent within a simulator.

\para Two distinct permanent-identifier values were generated by the field-programmable gate array on two power cycles during the validation session, each value produced by the field-programmable gate array's on-die true-random-number generator passed thru the on-die cryptographic conditioner during the chip-internal provisioning ceremony. The two values are cryptographically independent of each other and represent the chip's own entropy rather than entropy injected from outside the chip. The mirror-XOR line code was previously validated bit-exact in a prior validation session: 256 of 256 bidirectional round-trip exchanges completed without bit error over the single conductor between the field-programmable gate array and the microcontroller.

\para A fault-injection bitstream that bakes a single storage pair as burned and three storage pairs as kore was loaded onto the field-programmable gate array to simulate an interrupted provisioning. The chip's classifier identified the state correctly: chip\_state reported NGARO, wire output was gated off, the LED indicator confirmed the failure mode by a specific blink pattern, and the mirror-XOR heartbeat was absent from the wire. The byzantine quorum was below threshold; the chip refused to operate; the wire was silent. The observed behaviour matches the NGARO state property recited in \Clm{silentfail} on real silicon.

\subsection{Implementation Variations}

\para Validation on field-programmable gate array fabric, as described in the preceding section, employs implementation choices that are strictly easier problems on production application-specific integrated circuit silicon. The validation evidence is therefore a lower bound on what production silicon may achieve.

\para With respect to true-random-number generation primitives, the field-programmable gate array implementation uses ring-oscillator primitives only. Field-programmable gate array fabric does not natively support the metastability-based, transition-effect-ring-oscillator-based, or subthreshold-inverter-shot-noise-based primitives that production complementary metal-oxide semiconductor silicon supports. Production silicon embodiments may include any combination of the foregoing primitives in addition to ring oscillators, providing additional physics-grade entropy sources beyond what the field-programmable gate array can exercise.

\para With respect to write-once storage, the field-programmable gate array implementation emulates the inverted-pair storage layout using bitstream-initialized registers. Production silicon embodiments employ literal write-once storage cells, such as electrically programmable fuses, antifuses, mask read-only memory, or other write-once technologies, and provide electrical irreversibility by physical mechanism rather than by policy.

\para With respect to wire input/output, the field-programmable gate array development arrangement uses the field-programmable gate array's general-purpose input/output with internal weak pull-down as an approximation to the asymmetric drive arrangement. Production silicon embodiments provide a dedicated pin with a properly sized analog pull-down resistor.

\para With respect to slot rate, the field-programmable gate array implementation operates at a slot rate of approximately 50 kilohertz, this rate being the highest rate at which the microcontroller's clock and data recovery routine reliably locks. Production silicon embodiments include dedicated physical-layer logic with no equivalent slot-rate ceiling.

\para With respect to power gating of true-random-number generation primitives, field-programmable gate array fabric does not provide block-level power gating. Production silicon embodiments power-gate the true-random-number generation primitive domain, eliminating static power leakage and side-channel emanation of the primitive's oscillation frequency between entropy events.

\section{Abstract of the Disclosure}

A method and apparatus for vendor-free domain-separated cryptographic device attestation. An integrated-circuit chip generates a permanent identifier from physical entropy captured by on-chip noise-detection circuitry at the moment of an owner-initiated provisioning ceremony, conditions it thru an on-chip cascade of a chaotic-dynamical amplifier and a cryptographic hash, and atomically burns it to write-once storage under an energy-budgeted commit gate. No third party witnesses or signs the provisioning, and the identifier never leaves the chip. Attestations are keyed commitments over an application-supplied domain identifier, cryptographically independent across applications, preventing structural cross-application correlation. A volatile per-session secret, established once per power-on cycle and irrecoverably lost on power interruption, provides session-continuity proof and hardware-rooted provable erasure of session-bound capabilities. The chip's only interface is a single bidirectional pin carrying a mirror-XOR line code; identity storage uses inverted-pair write-once cells with byzantine quorum recovery; every failure mode presents wire silence indistinguishable from physical absence.
